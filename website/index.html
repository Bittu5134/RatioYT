Like/dislike ratings, back on YouTube.

RatioYT puts a like/dislike ratio on every YouTube video card — home, search, Shorts, playlists and queues — and can quietly blur the low-rated ones. Scores are cached on your device, so browsing stays fast.

Features

Score badges
A like/dislike ratio on video cards across home, search, Shorts, playlists and queues.
Dynamic colour
Each badge runs from red to green with the score, so a bad video reads at a glance.
Quiet mode
Hides the badges for a clean feed while scores are still fetched in the background.
Low-rated blur
Videos under your minimum score (set from 0–100%) are covered by a blur that also blocks hover previews. It stays until you turn the setting off.

Score formats

Four ways to read the same number. Colour runs red to green across all of them.

Format Example Notes
Percentage 73% Like share of all votes
Out of 10 7/10 Same score, rescaled
Smiley 😊 😐 😞 Good at 70+, mixed at 40+, poor below
Stars ★★★½☆ Five-star scale, half-star steps

How it works

Score source
Ratings come from the third-party Return YouTube Dislike API. Accuracy and uptime depend on that service.
Local cache
Fetched scores are stored in your browser's IndexedDB for seven days, so repeat views don't hit the network.
Selector updates
A remote config keeps the extension's YouTube selectors current, so UI changes don't require a new release.
Privacy
No telemetry, trackers or analytics. The only outbound requests are score lookups and that config refresh.

FAQ

Where do the scores come from?

From the public Return YouTube Dislike API, which estimates like/dislike counts from archived data and community contributions. RatioYT does not calculate ratings itself.

Does it send my viewing data anywhere?

No. There is no telemetry, tracking or analytics. The only outbound requests are score lookups and the selector-config refresh described above.

Why is a video blurred, and how do I watch it?

Videos scoring below your minimum threshold are covered by an overlay. It is not clickable on purpose — the point is to keep them out of the way. Lower the threshold or turn the setting off to watch them.

How do I report a bug or a wrong score?

Open an issue on the GitHub repository. Score accuracy issues usually trace back to the upstream API.
